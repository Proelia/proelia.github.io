ESFT — technical notes

Flux-based spacetime model, working equations, and empirical methodology notes. Provisional research — not peer-reviewed final claims.

Scope: Engineering / physics audience. Non-specialists should start with the plain-language dossier.

Core constructs

Gravity as flux flow

Masses induce density gradients in a flux substrate; gravity is treated as directed flow redistributing energy.

$$\mathbf{v} \propto -\nabla \rho$$

Time as energy gradient

Local time linked to dissipation rate in the flux; arrow of time as gradient-flattening tendency.

Expansion via eddies

Microscopic gravitational eddies from baryonic motion contribute an effective energy term driving medium expansion.

$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3c^2} (\rho_m + \rho_r + \rho_{eddy})$$

Empirical methodology

  • Bayesian MCMC parameter estimation (e.g. emcee)
  • Fits against public probes (e.g. Pantheon+ supernovae; related cosmological datasets)
  • Model comparison via information criteria (e.g. AIC) against baseline expansion histories

Early results suggest broad expansion history can be tracked in tested regimes; distinctive high-redshift behaviour remains the primary discriminant to stress-test.
