Emergent Spacetime Flux Theory

Independent research exploring a different way to think about how the universe expands and forms structure — written so non-specialists can follow the idea.

The simple idea: Much of modern cosmology explains the universe with “dark” ingredients we have not seen directly. ESFT asks whether spacetime itself can be pictured as a kind of energetic medium — a flux — and whether gravity, time, and expansion can be read as behaviour of that medium.

What problem is it aiming at?

Textbooks often say most of the universe is dark matter and dark energy. Those labels work well in equations, but they are placeholders for things we have not observed the way we observe stars and gas. ESFT is one independent attempt to explore alternatives grounded in a fluid-like picture of spacetime.

Three everyday metaphors

  • Gravity as flow — masses create “slopes” in the medium; things move as the medium redistributes.
  • Time as change in energy — local time is tied to how the medium settles and dissipates.
  • Expansion as many tiny swirls — ordinary motion throughout the cosmos adds up to a swelling effect in the medium.

These are research metaphors, not proven facts.

Has it been tested?

Early numerical work compares the model to public astronomical datasets (for example supernova distance data) using statistical fitting methods. So far, the work suggests it can track broad expansion history in the regimes checked. That is a starting point, not a final victory.

Honest status: This is early-stage independent research. It is not peer-reviewed gospel. Every claim should be treated as provisional until stronger, independent validation.

What it is not

  • Not a finished theory of everything
  • Not a product or investment pitch
  • Not a claim that dark matter “doesn’t exist” as an observational phenomenon — the project is about modelling frameworks
