Emergent Spacetime Flux Theory

Independent physics research exploring whether the expanding universe and its structure can be described with a dynamic “flux” picture of spacetime — without leaning only on unseen dark components. Early-stage. Claims stay provisional.

In one sentence: ESFT treats spacetime as an emergent energetic medium; gravity as flow, time as local dissipation, and accelerated expansion as the cumulative effect of microscopic motion “eddies” in that medium.

The problem it tries to address

Standard cosmology explains most of the universe’s energy budget with dark matter and dark energy — components that are not directly observed. ESFT asks whether a flux-based spacetime model can reproduce key expansion history without treating those components as fundamental.

Core ideas (technical)

Gravity as flux flow

Masses induce density gradients in the flux substrate. “Gravity” is the directed flow that redistributes energy toward lower gradients — a fluid analogue rather than primary geometric curvature.

$$\mathbf{v} \propto -\nabla \rho$$

Time as energy gradient

Local time is linked to the rate of energy dissipation in the flux, giving a physical reading of the arrow of time as the medium’s tendency to flatten gradients.

Expansion via eddies

Microscopic gravitational “eddies” from baryonic motion (from atomic scales upward) are treated as a cumulative energy term that can drive effective expansion of the medium.

$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3c^2} (\rho_m + \rho_r + \rho_{eddy})$$

How it was checked so far

  • Bayesian MCMC — parameter estimation (e.g. density terms) with libraries such as emcee
  • Public datasets — fits against supernova compilations (e.g. Pantheon+) and related cosmological probes
  • Model comparison — information criteria (e.g. AIC) to compare goodness-of-fit against baseline histories

Early numerical work suggests the framework can track broad expansion history comparable to ΛCDM in the regimes tested; distinctive high-redshift predictions remain the interesting test cases.

Status: Research notes and numerical experiments — not a published peer-reviewed theory of everything. Treat every claim as provisional until independent replication and formal publication.
